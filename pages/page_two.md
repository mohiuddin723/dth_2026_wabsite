# Disadvantages of Open Source Software

Free to download doesn't imply free to maintain. Programmers have to correct errors, check modifications, compose documentation and respond to inquiries. The demands can be too much for unpaid volunteers. GitHub's guidance recognises maintainer burnout and the need to share responsibilities (GitHub, n.d.). When a key maintainer departs, users may be left with outdated or no software changes.

Software security is not a given with public code. It needs to be checked by someone and vulnerabilities fixed. The support of the community might also not be timely enough for a student with a deadline. These risks are different—some projects are staffed with paid personnel and others are dependent on volunteers with a few hours to spare. Users should evaluate actual maintenance arrangements of the project.

The cost of adoption should be taken into account by organisations. Training and migration support and document-compatibility checks may be required for a municipality that is transitioning to LibreOffice. The Document Foundation (n.d.) has a list of professionals who offer these services. The savings on licence, thus, do not eliminate all costs. A responsible decision takes into account the staff capacity, support and compatibility in addition to the price. A free licence is only useful if the software is appropriate for the users.
